## Scope and route assumptions

These findings identify documented hawthorns, offering trees, ringforts, dolmens, and related folklore sites near the itinerary. Likely road corridors were inferred from the planned stops, not checked as a live navigation route. Boyle–Foxford and the approach to Downpatrick Head have alternative routes, so some candidates depend on the road chosen. The precise Sligo routing remains unresolved.

Camper parking, turning space, current land access, and precise driving detours have not been verified unless specifically stated. Suggested visit allowances are planning judgments, not confirmed opening hours. A monument record does not grant public access across farmland. No bookings, enquiries, or itinerary changes were made through this research.

## Priorities

1. **Creevykeel Court Tomb and entrance wishing trees:** the strongest roadside match, beside the N15.
2. **Doonfeeny Standing Stone:** a short geographical addition near Downpatrick Head.
3. **Dolmen of the Four Maols:** directly relevant to the existing Ballina stop.
4. **Oweynagat:** the clearest documented hawthorn-and-Otherworld match; arrange the visit through Rathcroghan Visitor Centre.
5. **Drumanone Portal Tomb:** a standout dolmen near Boyle, conditional on resolving current access.

## Finds in travel order

### Roscommon area — November 3 or another Roscommon visit

#### Oweynagat — Cave of the Cats, near Tulsk

- **Type:** cave entrance beneath a documented hawthorn; associated with the Otherworld and the Morrígan in medieval stories.
- **Route fit:** an intentional excursion toward Tulsk, rather than a quick roadside photograph on the camper pickup route.
- **Access:** arrange through Rathcroghan Visitor Centre. Confirm whether the selected tour visits the entrance or enters the cave; the operator lists different experiences. The seasonal “Origins of Samhain” description explicitly excludes cave entry.
- **Scheduling:** the regular winter guided tour is listed at 11:30 on weekdays and lasts approximately 2½ hours. This requires a deliberate adjustment around the fixed camper pickup or return.
- **Sources:** [Oweynagat archaeology and folklore](https://www.tuatha.ie/oweynagat-cave/), [official guided tours](https://www.rathcroghan.ie/discover/guided-tours/), [seasonal entrance-only tour description](https://www.rathcroghan.ie/tours-events/halloween-at-rathcroghan/).

#### Rathmore and the Rathcroghan earthworks

- **Type:** substantial circular earthwork within the royal archaeological landscape, alongside mounds and other enclosures.
- **Route fit:** combine with an Oweynagat/Rathcroghan excursion.
- **Experience:** best explored with interpretation; otherwise many features can appear to be ordinary grassy fields.
- **Access:** ask which monuments the particular tour physically visits. The operator lists Rathmore among monuments discussed; that does not guarantee access to every listed site.
- **Source:** [Rathcroghan guided tour information](https://www.rathcroghan.ie/discover/guided-tours/).

### Boyle departure — November 4

#### Drumanone Portal Tomb — also called Tinnacarra Dolmen

- **Type:** large portal tomb with a huge capstone and imposing upright stones.
- **Location:** approximately 3 km west of Boyle, near the R294.
- **Route fit:** close to the start of a westbound departure from Boyle, particularly if the R294 route is chosen. Preserve the booked 11:30 Foxford experience.
- **Access unresolved:** published directions describe a farm lane and railway crossing. Those descriptions do not establish current permission or a safe public approach. Confirm an authorized approach before attempting the visit; do not rely on older instructions to cross the railway.
- **Sources:** [Monumental Ireland description](https://monumentalireland.ie/monument-details/?monument_id=1168), [published field description and access account](https://www.megalithicireland.com/Drumanone.htm).

#### Kilcashel Stone Fort, near Kilmovee

- **Type:** substantial circular stone ringfort with a lintelled entrance, wall chambers, and remains of houses.
- **Route fit:** only a candidate if choosing the Ballaghaderreen/Kilmovee side of the Boyle–Foxford journey. It is a meaningful detour, not an automatic stop on every westbound route.
- **Scheduling:** difficult to add casually before the fixed Foxford booking.
- **Access unresolved:** current land access, approach and camper parking need confirmation.
- **Source:** [Our Irish Heritage — Kilcashel Stone Fort](https://www.ouririshheritage.org/content/archive/place/miscellaneous-place/kilcashel-stone-fort-2).

### Mulranny/Tiernaur area — November 4

#### Blea na Shee — local fairy-fort lead

- **Type:** explicitly named as a fairy fort in Mulranny’s community heritage and Clew Bay walking material.
- **Route fit:** the right general area for the proposed Nevin’s overnight.
- **Location/access unresolved:** the published walking description covers a 19 km guided outing and does not establish a precise, short public approach to the fort. Do not treat the name alone as a verified navigation pin.
- **Next step:** ask Mulranny tourist information or a local walking guide to identify the site and an authorized short approach.
- **Sources:** [Mulranny walking information](https://mulranny.ie/walking/), [community heritage plan](https://mulranny.ie/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Mulranny-Community-Futures-Action-Plan.pdf).

### Downpatrick Head, Killala and Ballina — November 5

#### Doonfeeny Standing Stone

- **Type:** roughly 4.5-metre standing stone, later marked with Christian crosses.
- **Location:** about 3.5 km west of Ballycastle. The local tourism account places the stone in the newer graveyard across the road from the ruined church.
- **Route fit:** one of the best small additions to the Downpatrick Head day.
- **Experience:** a striking stone in a coastal setting with views toward Downpatrick Head. The church ruins themselves are enclosed by a protective barrier.
- **Access:** current camper parking and turning space are unverified. Use the established graveyard entrance and respect the burial ground.
- **Source:** [Visit North Mayo — Doonfeeny Church and Standing Stone](https://www.northmayo.ie/doonfeeny-church-and-standing-stone/).

#### Rathfranpark Wedge Tomb and Rathfran archaeological complex

- **Type:** wedge tomb, with a nearby state-recorded complex including stone circle, mound, ringfort, souterrain, and enclosure.
- **Location:** off the R314 north of Killala; the wedge tomb is described beside a minor road.
- **Route fit:** most useful if taking the R314 toward Killala and Ballina after Downpatrick Head. It may require a different detour if returning inland via Crossmolina.
- **Access:** the archaeological inventory confirms the monuments, not that every feature is visible, open, or easily accessible. Current field access and camper stopping arrangements need checking.
- **Sources:** [Rathfranpark tomb and location](https://www.ouririshheritage.org/content/archive/place/miscellaneous-place/rathfranpark-wedge-tomb), [National Monuments inventory for Mayo](https://www.archaeology.ie/app/uploads/2025/03/monuments-in-state-care-mayo.pdf).

#### Dolmen of the Four Maols — Table of the Giants

- **Type:** capstone monument traditionally known as a dolmen.
- **Location:** Primrose Hill behind Ballina railway station.
- **Route fit:** one of the simplest geographical additions, because Ballina is already on the itinerary.
- **Folklore:** the name is linked to a story of four clerics who murdered a bishop and were subsequently killed. Treat that as a tradition attached to the monument, rather than a secure explanation of its construction.
- **Access:** current walking approach and camper parking need verification.
- **Sources:** [Mayo County Library — Dolmens](https://www.mayo.ie/library/local-history/historical-events/early-mayo/dolmens), [North Mayo heritage newsletter with location](https://northmayoheritagecentre.ie/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Newsletter-6th-july-2025-FINAL.pdf).

### West Sligo and Sligo approach — November 5–6

#### Split Rock, Killeenduff, near Easkey

- **Type:** glacial boulder split in two, with giant folklore.
- **Location:** the local community places it about a mile south of Easkey on the R297.
- **Folklore:** traditionally split during a dispute between giants, including Fionn MacCumhaill. Another story says the rock will close on someone who passes through the split three times.
- **Route fit:** useful if choosing the coastal R297 alternative toward west Sligo; not directly on the inland N59.
- **Access:** current field access and a suitable camper stopping place have not been established.
- **Source:** [Easkey local sights](https://easkey.ie/attractions-activities-easkey/local-sights/).

#### Tobernalt Holy Well and rag trees

- **Type:** wooded holy spring with a documented devotional offering-tree tradition.
- **Location:** near Carraroe and the southwest corner of Lough Gill, outside Sligo; a diversion from the N4 approach.
- **Route fit:** a sacred-tree candidate while travelling through the Sligo area.
- **Tree identification:** offering or rag trees are documented here. The species of the current offering tree has not been verified; do not describe it as a confirmed individual fairy hawthorn.
- **Access:** an established place of pilgrimage maintained by local parishioners. Current camper parking capacity remains unverified.
- **Sources:** [Parish history of Tobernalt](https://holywellsligo.com/about-tobernalt/), [local heritage account of the rag-tree tradition](https://carrowkeel.com/sites/coolrea/tobernalt.html).

#### The Giant’s Griddle, Tawnatruffan — more demanding candidate

- **Type:** striking portal dolmen associated in folklore with the Fianna and their cooking.
- **Location:** south of Dromore West, in the upper Easkey river valley near the Ox Mountains.
- **Route fit:** geographically relevant to the Sligo area, but not established as a short roadside detour.
- **Access unresolved:** the local account describes back roads, forestry and difficulty finding the monument. Current permission, walking route, ground conditions, and camper approach require further checking.
- **Source:** [Dromore West — The Giant’s Griddle](https://dromorewest.ie/history/the-giants-griddle/).

### N15 near Cliffoney — November 6 northbound or November 7 southbound

#### Creevykeel Court Tomb and entrance wishing trees

- **Type:** large Neolithic court tomb with an open court and burial chambers; rag or wishing trees are documented along the entrance.
- **Location:** beside the N15 near Cliffoney, about 2 km northeast of the village according to Heritage Ireland.
- **Route fit:** the strongest roadside match. Likely relevant both on the journey toward Donegal and the return toward Sligo/Galway.
- **Visit allowance:** roughly 20–30 minutes as a planning judgment.
- **Access:** a small visitor car park and short walk are documented. Camper space, height restrictions and turning arrangements have not been checked. Assess the available space before entering.
- **Tree identification:** sources document the offering trees; the species and condition of each current tree have not been independently checked.
- **Sources:** [Heritage Ireland monument description](https://heritageireland.ie/ga/suimh-neamhthreoraithe/creevykeel-court-tomb/), [entrance trees and parking description](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/creevykeel-court-tomb-sligo-ireland).

- **Location:** northwest of Ballyshannon, in Kilbarron townland, near the road toward Rossnowlagh/Creevy.
- **Route fit:** close enough to investigate from Ballyshannon, but not a verified casual visitor stop.
- **Access unresolved:** current field access, visibility, walking approach, parking and turning space are unknown.

#### Shalwy and Croaghbeg Court Tombs — difficult November candidates

- **Type:** substantial court tombs near the coastal road between Killybegs and Kilcar.
- **Route fit:** relevant if taking that coastal approach to or from Slieve League.
- **Access concerns:** published field accounts describe marshy ground and difficult access, including inability to reach Croaghbeg on one visit. These accounts are not current permission or condition reports.
- **Recommendation:** favour Creevykeel for this trip, given November conditions and the Ballyshannon concert deadline.
- **Sources:** [Field description of Shalwy and Croaghbeg](https://megalithicireland.com/Shalwy.htm), [Croaghbeg monument description](https://monumentalireland.ie/monument-details/?monument_id=1011).

### Galway approach — November 7, or a separately planned excursion

#### Knockma Hill, near Castlehackett

- **Type:** fairy landscape with cairns and woodland walking, traditionally associated with Finnvara, king of the Connacht fairies, and Queen Maeve.
- **Location:** west of Tuam, around Castlehackett/Caherlistrane.
- **Route fit:** a diversion from the likely N17/Tuam approach to Galway. It is an outing, not a five-minute roadside stop.
- **Visit allowance:** main loop approximately 4.2 km; local visitor information suggests 1–1½ hours, plus the driving diversion.
- **Scheduling:** the itinerary’s early Galway parking target makes it difficult to add on November 7 without changing that target.
- **Access:** visitor parking and established trails are documented; camper suitability is not verified. Castlehackett House is not open to the public.
- **Sources:** [Discover Ireland — folklore and visitor access](https://www.discoverireland.ie/galway/knockma-hill-hill-of-maeve), [Galway Tourism — trail details](https://www.galwaytourism.ie/knockma-hill/).

### Galway–Roscommon — November 9

#### Castlestrange Stone, near Athleague/Fuerty

- **Type:** granite boulder with flowing Iron Age La Tène carvings. It is not a dolmen or fairy fort, but is a good related ancient-site candidate.
- **Location:** beside a drive in the grounds of Castlestrange, near Athleague.
- **Route fit:** a possible diversion around the Roscommon end of the Galway–Roscommon journey, depending on the selected road.
- **Access:** private grounds; confirm the present visitor approach and avoid obstructing the entrance or farm access. Camper parking and turning space remain unverified.
- **Sources:** [Monumental Ireland description](https://monumentalireland.ie/monument-details/?monument_id=1166), [Roscommon County Council heritage guide](https://www.roscommoncoco.ie/en/Download-It/Heritage-Publications/Heritage-in-Co-Roscommon-Where-to-go-What-to-See-.pdf).

## Hawthorn and folklore distinctions

**Oweynagat is the clearest documented hawthorn match.** Creevykeel and Tobernalt provide documented offering-tree traditions. An offering tree is not automatically a hawthorn, and a holy-well tradition is not automatically a fairy-tree tradition.

Historical fairy-tree accounts were also found in the folklore archive. An old account alone does not establish that its tree survives or that a modern visitor can locate or access it. These have not been promoted as confirmed stops.

The archaeological type and folklore should remain distinct: a ringfort, tomb, natural cave, or glacial boulder can acquire later supernatural stories without those stories establishing its original purpose.

## Before adding stops to the itinerary

- Confirm an authorized approach to **Drumanone**, particularly the railway issue.
- Ask Rathcroghan Visitor Centre which experience fits the available time and whether it includes **Oweynagat’s entrance or cave entry**.
- Obtain a precise location and permitted short approach for **Blea na Shee** from local visitor information or a guide.
- Choose the **Boyle–Foxford**, **Downpatrick Head–Ballina**, and **Ballina–west Sligo** road variants before calculating detours.
- Check camper stopping and turning space at selected sites; a car-park listing does not establish motorhome suitability.
- Use established approaches, respect farmland and burial grounds, leave stones and trees untouched, and avoid adding ribbons or other objects.
