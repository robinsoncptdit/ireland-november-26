Researched October 15, 2026. Companion to [Wanderlog Itinerary.md](itinerary.md), the local reference for trip dates and places.

These are ranked options around each stop or cluster of nearby stops, not a plan to complete every activity. The shorter stops will not accommodate all five. Recommendations reflect current venue websites, heritage notices, local tourism sources, menus, and November event programmes. Published hours do not confirm availability on a particular date. No reservations have been made through this research.

## Strongest overall picks

The Shed Distillery, the booked Foxford experience, Rosserk Friary, a Sligo seaweed bath, Slieve League, Galway’s art and music weekend, and 14 Henrietta Street in Dublin.

## 1. Athlone — November 2 and 10

### Five things to do

1. **Sean’s Bar:** the essential Athlone pub experience. Go for its history, a pint, and whatever music is happening; it also offers whiskey and Irish coffee experiences. [Sean’s Bar](https://www.seansbar.ie/)
2. **Luan Gallery:** contemporary art overlooking the Shannon. It closes Mondays, so **November 10 is the better date**. [Gallery hours](https://luangallery.ie/)
3. **Saints Peter and Paul’s Church:** a short stop for the Harry Clarke Studio stained glass, easily paired with the riverside. [Tourism Ireland](https://www.ireland.com/en/magazine/guides/athlone/)
4. **Old Rail Trail:** walk a short section rather than committing to a big cycle. An easy way to get outside after travel. [Trail information](https://www.visitwestmeath.ie/get-outdoors/cycle-the-greenways/old-rail-trail-greenway/)
5. **Portlick Millennium Forest:** a quieter excursion with native woodland and Lough Ree views. Requires transport from town; the full trail is 5 km, with shorter alternatives. [Portlick](https://www.visitwestmeath.ie/see-and-do/walks-wilderness/portlick-millennium-forest/)

### Eat and drink

**Sean’s Bar** is the drinks priority. **Bacchus** is a practical riverside dinner choice on either date, with seafood, grilled meats, pasta, and wine; its published schedule includes Monday and Tuesday evenings. [Bacchus](https://www.bacchusrestaurant.ie/about)

**Date constraints:** Dead Centre Brewing closes Monday and Tuesday, missing both stays. Athlone Castle’s own update places reopening likely in 2027. [Brewery hours](https://deadcentrebrewing.com/plan-your-visit/), [castle update](https://www.athlonecastle.ie/news/)

## 2. Roscommon and surroundings — November 3 and 9–10

### Five things to do

1. **Rathcroghan:** royal archaeology, Queen Medb, and stories of the Irish Otherworld. The regular winter tour runs weekdays at 11:30 and lasts about 2½ hours. **Requires a deliberate schedule change**, rather than fitting around camper collection or return. [Tour details](https://www.rathcroghan.ie/visit-planning/opening-times-facilities/)
2. **Roscommon Castle and Loughnaneane Park:** the strongest easy addition close to town; choose this when there is an hour rather than half a day. [Roscommon heritage information](https://www.visitroscommon.ie/plan-your-visit/)
3. **Strokestown Park and the National Famine Museum:** a substantial history visit, particularly compelling with the house’s upstairs/downstairs story. Current information lists daily opening and house tours at noon and 14:00. [Strokestown](https://strokestownpark.ie/)
4. **Mote Park:** woodland walking with estate history; a low-pressure alternative to another indoor attraction. [Coillte](https://www.coillte.ie/site/mote-park/)
5. **Roscommon Abbey:** a shorter heritage stop to pair with the castle and town centre. [Local visitor directory](https://www.visitroscommon.ie/plan-your-visit/)

### Eat and drink

**Gleeson’s** is the first food choice: locally sourced cooking, house-baked bread, and a welcoming townhouse setting. Its published menu includes seafood chowder with soda bread. **JJ Harlow’s** is the pub candidate for a traditional interior and a pint; music for Monday evening has not been confirmed. [Gleeson’s](https://gleesonsroscommon.ie/restaurant/), [JJ Harlow’s recent visitor information](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g315887-d4289763-Reviews-JJ_Harlows-Roscommon_County_Roscommon_Western_Ireland.html)

## 3. Carrick-on-Shannon, Battlebridge and Drumshanbo — November 3

### Five things to do

1. **The Shed Distillery:** probably the most distinctive individual attraction on this stretch. A working distillery producing Drumshanbo Gunpowder Gin and single pot still whiskey, with tastings in its botanical glasshouse. Allow **at least 90 minutes**. [The Shed](https://www.thesheddistillery.com/visit-us/)
2. **Acres Lake floating boardwalk:** the best short outdoor stop—an unusual walk right over the water. [Shannon Blueway information](https://www.discoverireland.ie/irelands-hidden-heartlands/free-things-to-do)
3. **Battlebridge–Drumleague Blueway:** sample a short waterside section on foot rather than trying to complete a cycling outing after camper handover. [Blueway](https://www.discoverireland.ie/irelands-hidden-heartlands/free-things-to-do)
4. **Costello Memorial Chapel:** a tiny, beautifully detailed memorial built by a husband for his wife. An excellent brief stop in Carrick. [Chapel history](https://www.leitrimireland.com/listings/costello-memorial-chapel/)
5. **The Dock:** galleries in a former courthouse—a good rainy-day choice. Published gallery hours are Tuesday–Saturday, 10:00–17:00. [The Dock](https://www.thedock.ie/about/policies)

### Eat and drink

**The Oarsman** is the destination restaurant here, celebrating seasonal regional produce, wine, craft beer, and spirits. On Tuesday it opens **at 17:00**, so dinner means reaching Boyle later. The Shed’s **Gunpowder G&T** is the signature local drink; the driver should leave tasting until driving is finished. [The Oarsman](https://theoarsman.com/)

**Planning judgment:** this afternoon has room for the distillery or several short stops. Combining the full tour, cycling, a leisurely dinner, and the drive to Boyle would be too much.

## 4. Boyle — November 3–4

### Five things to do

1. **Lough Key woodland and lakeshore:** the best uncomplicated outdoor choice, especially with daylight before leaving.
2. **King House:** Georgian grandeur, the King family, and Connaught Rangers history. **November visits need advance arrangement**; ordinary tours finish at Halloween. [King House](https://www.visitkinghouse.ie/visit-us-explore/plan-your-visit/opening-times-and-prices/)
3. **Arigna Mining Experience:** a regional detour for underground industrial history. It publishes year-round daily tours lasting 45 minutes, but this needs additional driving time. [Arigna](https://arignaminingexperience.ie/contact-us/)
4. **Boyle Experience Trail:** explore the town’s history and old streets without another scheduled attraction. [Town trail](https://www.visitroscommon.ie/boyle-experience-trail/)
5. **Úna Bhán craft shop:** local makers and souvenirs in the grounds of King House, with year-round visitor information. [Úna Bhán](https://discoverboyle.ie/contact/)

### Eat and drink

**King House Tea Rooms** is the daytime food candidate, subject to service hours. For Tuesday evening, investigate **Creighton’s Bar** for traditional character or **The Patrick’s Well**; both have published weekday evening hours. [Food directory](https://discoverboyle.ie/listing-category/food_and_drink/), [Boyle pub directory](https://www.visitroscommon.ie/boyle-experience-trail/)

**November constraint:** Lough Key’s visitor facilities and attractions close Monday and Tuesday in November, although the estate remains open. Wednesday opening at 10:00 conflicts with the Foxford morning, so focus on the grounds. [Lough Key hours](https://loughkey.ie/opening-times/)

## 5. Foxford, Westport, Newport and Mulranny — November 4

### Five things to do

1. **The booked Foxford experience:** preserve this anchor. Seeing the looms and hearing the mill’s story gives the shopping a connection to the place. The local itinerary records two people booked at 11:30, free of charge.
2. **Westport town and the Quay:** browse the streets, then choose either a town lunch or a waterfront pause.
3. **Burrishoole Abbey:** an atmospheric small addition beside an estuary, just off the Newport–Mulranny road. Allow roughly 30–45 minutes. [Burrishoole](https://www.discoverireland.ie/mayo/burrishoole-abbey)
4. **Mulranny causeway and shore:** a compact way to experience Clew Bay without the long Achill detour.
5. **A short Great Western Greenway outing:** pick a section around Newport or Mulranny rather than treating it as a full cycling day. [Local walking information](https://mulranny.ie/walking/)

### Eat and drink

- **Foxford Café:** the strongest lunch recommendation for this day. Seasonal soups, salads, hot dishes, and baking made on site; Wednesday café hours are 10:00–16:00. [Foxford Café](https://www.foxford.com/pages/foxford-cafe)
- **The Creel at Westport Quay:** an alternative for brunch, lunch, or takeaway supplies. [Westport food guide](https://www.destinationwestport.com/explore-westport/a-locals-guide-to-the-westport-food-scene)
- **Nevin’s:** the practical dinner choice near the proposed overnight; its current site lists daily food service and dishes including cod and chips and lamb shank. [Nevin’s](https://www.nevinsinn.com/bar-restaurant/)
- **Matt Molloy’s:** the standout music pub, but the experience is an evening session. Enjoying that properly requires changing the overnight or arranging transport. [Westport music guide](https://www.destinationwestport.com/explore-westport/top-five-live-music-pubs-in-westport)

## 6. Downpatrick Head, Ballina and Enniscrone — November 5

### Five things to do

1. **Downpatrick Head:** the landscape priority—Dún Briste sea stack, Atlantic cliffs, and the blowhole. The tourism board suggests 30–60 minutes. [Downpatrick Head](https://www.discoverireland.ie/mayo/downpatrick-head)
2. **Rosserk Friary:** a quieter alternative or companion stop, with Gothic ruins beside the Moy estuary. Access is during daylight. [Heritage Ireland](https://heritageireland.ie/unguided-sites/rosserk-friary/)
3. **Belleek Castle:** the most unusual indoor option, with castle interiors, armoury, and museum tours; confirm tour timing. [Belleek Castle](https://belleekcastle.com/)
4. **Jackie Clarke Collection:** Irish history through one collector’s remarkable archive; a strong wet-weather option in Ballina. [Collection visitor guide](https://www.clarkecollection.ie/images/resources/JackieClarke_Social_story_.pdf)
5. **Enniscrone beach:** a broad, windswept beach walk on the way toward west Sligo. [Enniscrone information](https://www.discoverireland.ie/guides/swimming-in-the-sea)

### Eat and drink

**Jack Fenn Café & Bistro at Belleek Castle** is the first lunch choice: fresh baking, sandwiches, seafood, and a distinctive setting. It fits the Sligo arrival more naturally than a lengthy castle dinner. [Belleek dining](https://belleekcastle.com/dining/)

**Seasonal constraint:** Céide Fields’ regular 2026 season ends November 4. From November 5, it is available through winter bookings rather than ordinary opening. [Official dates](https://heritageireland.ie/places-to-visit/ceide-fields-neolithic-site-visitor-centre/)

## 7. West Sligo, Strandhill and Sligo town — November 5

### Five things to do

1. **VOYA seaweed bath, Strandhill:** a standout new experience to consider—private steam and a hot seaweed bath after coastal driving. Published hours are daily, 09:45–18:00. [VOYA](https://www.voyaseaweedbaths.com/contact/)
2. **Carrowmore:** a major prehistoric landscape with a newly upgraded visitor centre. Its published 2026 season continues to December 2. [Carrowmore](https://heritageireland.ie/visit/places-to-visit/carrowmore-megalithic-cemetery/)
3. **Dunmoran Strand and Aughris:** the most natural coastal choice around west Sligo. Take a short beach walk rather than committing to the entire route. [Sligo walking guide](https://sligowalks.ie/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Sligo-Walking-Guide.pdf)
4. **The Model:** free art galleries and the Niland Collection; an indoor alternative. [The Model](https://www.themodel.ie/visit/)
5. **Knocknarea’s Queen Maeve Trail:** the ambitious option for views and archaeology. It deserves a substantial daylight window and suitable weather; adding it to the existing driving day needs careful consideration. [Trail directory](https://sligowalks.ie/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Sligo-Walking-Guide.pdf)

### Eat and drink

**Shells in Strandhill** for seaside food, baking, and coffee; **Hargadon Bros in Sligo** for an old grocery-pub interior, local cooking, Guinness, or wine. Both involve going beyond the Sligo area. [Shells](https://www.shellscafe.com/pages/cafe), [Hargadon Bros](https://www.hargadons.com/welcome)

**Thursday food constraint:** Aughris’s Beach Bar looks ideal, but the tourism listing says low-season food is served at weekends. Do not rely on it for Thursday dinner. [Beach Bar information](https://www.discoverireland.ie/sligo/the-beach-bar)

## 8. Slieve League and Ballyshannon — November 6

### Five things to do

1. **Slieve League’s main viewpoints:** keep the cliff experience focused to leave by 14:00.
2. **A locally guided shuttle visit:** potentially the best upgrade to the cliffs, adding local history while simplifying the approach. The operator requests direct booking enquiries; November service still needs confirmation. [Cliffs Centre](https://www.slieveleague.com/tours)
3. **Ár Seisiún Mór at the Allingham Festival:** the evening anchor and strongest cultural experience of the day. The festival confirms its Friday, November 6 programme. Tickets remain outstanding in the local itinerary. [Allingham Festival](https://allinghamfestival.com/)
4. **Ballyshannon’s Rory Gallagher landmarks and town wander:** a short arrival activity before dinner. [Local attractions](https://discoverballyshannon.ie/product-category/attractions/)
5. **Fairy Bridges and Wishing Chair, Bundoran:** a regional extra if weather and daylight allow—natural sea arches with folklore attached. Treat this as an alternative to extra cliff time given the schedule. [Discover Bundoran](https://discoverbundoran.com/fairy-bridges-bundoran/)

### Eat and drink

- **Tí Linn Café at the Cliffs Centre:** the easiest lunch plan; the operator specifically recommends its Donegal chowder. [Cliffs Centre café](https://www.slieveleague.com/tours)
- **The Rusty Mackerel:** an atmospheric alternative near Teelin. Drinks include Silkie whiskey, An Dúlamán gin, and local craft beer—bottles to remember for later. [Rusty Mackerel](https://therustymackerel.com/)
- **Dicey Reilly’s in Ballyshannon:** an interesting town drinks stop once parked for the night. [Dicey’s](https://diceys.com/home)

Settle dinner near the concert before adding another coastal detour. Preserve the itinerary’s no-driving-after-concert plan.

## 9. Galway — November 7–9

### Five things to do

1. **Going Coastal’s Saturday market and performances:** a particularly good match for the dates. The Blue Note and Massimo programme starts at 13:00 November 7, with free music and art events.
2. **TULCA: Veruca Salt:** the contemporary-art priority, running November 6–22. [TULCA](https://www.tulca.ie/tulca-2026)
3. **Going Coastal’s Sunday music:** choose among Salthouse at 16:00, Plámás at 18:00, and the 19:00 Áras na nGael finale. The first two are free; the finale is listed at €5. [Published weekend programme](https://galwayswestend.ie/going-coastal-brings-three-days-of-independent-music-to-galways-westend-this-november/)
4. **A food walking tour:** worth considering if tasting several local producers matters more than another sightseeing circuit. Requires a reserved time block. [Tour & Taste Galway](https://www.discoverireland.ie/galway/tour-taste-galway)
5. **A slow city wander with a traditional-pub stop:** market streets, Spanish Arch, the waterfront, then a snug at Tigh Neachtain. A flexible option between festival commitments. [Winter Galway guide](https://www.discoverireland.ie/galway-city/winter-in-galway)

### Eat and drink

- **Kai:** the first dinner choice for seasonal food with personality. **Book Saturday—it closes Sunday and Monday.** Lunch is walk-in; dinner requires a reservation. [Kai](https://www.kairestaurant.ie/)
- **Ard Bia at Nimmos:** the first Sunday brunch choice, beside Spanish Arch; published weekend brunch is 10:00–15:00. [Ard Bia](https://www.ardbia.com/visit)
- **Tigh Neachtain:** protect time for its snugs, whiskey, craft beer, and lunch served daily 12:00–16:00. [Tigh Neachtain](https://www.tighneachtain.com/home)
- **Aniar:** the big culinary splurge, currently advertising a €175 tasting menu rooted in western Irish ingredients. Its website has conflicting opening-hour information, so confirm the service date before planning around it. [Menu](https://www.aniarrestaurant.ie/menu), [hours](https://www.aniarrestaurant.ie/location)

## 10. Dublin — November 11–12

### Five things to do

1. **14 Henrietta Street:** the strongest recommendation—a guided journey from Georgian wealth to crowded tenement life. Opens Wednesday–Sunday, matching both dates; booking is required. [Visitor information](https://www.dublincity.ie/archaeology-conservation-and-heritage/dublins-historic-buildings/14-henrietta-street)
2. **Kilmainham Gaol:** the other history priority. Allow about 90 minutes and use the official booking site. Tickets are released 28 days ahead, so the dates are entering the booking window as of October 15. [Kilmainham](https://www.kilmainhamgaolmuseum.ie/page/7/)
3. **National Museum—Archaeology:** bog bodies and extraordinary early Irish objects; particularly convenient to the city centre. [Museum collection guide](https://www.museum.ie/getmedia/a3ebbb22-2057-4200-aae8-cd958854d89f/ks_guide_web_en-%281%29.pdf)
4. **National Gallery:** an easy art visit close to central Dublin. Normally opens late Thursdays, although it currently warns of possible short-notice closures linked to industrial action. [Gallery hours](https://www.nationalgallery.ie/visit-us/opening-hours)
5. **The Cobblestone:** a music-first pub experience, with traditional sessions seven days a week. [Session information](https://www.cobblestonepub.ie/irish-traditional-music-sessions)

### Eat and drink

- **Delahunt:** the first dinner choice for Irish cooking in a characterful historic setting; its upstairs Sitting Room also makes a good cocktail stop. Published dinner service includes Wednesday and Thursday. [Delahunt](https://delahunt.ie/contact/)
- **Mr Fox:** the alternative destination dinner, with seasonal contemporary Irish food and a monthly changing menu. [Mr Fox](https://www.mrfox.ie/about/)
- **O’Donoghue’s on Merrion Row:** a convenient music-and-pint choice near central Dublin; published midweek music begins at 20:00. [Drinks and music information](https://www.odonoghues.ie/attachment/_843172b4518ad7f8_en_o_donoghues_drinks_list_jan2026.pdf)
- **The Palace Bar:** the whiskey stop, especially for its own bottlings and Victorian interior. [The Palace](https://www.thepalacebardublin.com/store)

**Closures covering the trip:** Chester Beatty is closed through December 2026, and Clonmacnoise is fully closed from October 19 into January 2027. Older guides still recommend both. [Chester Beatty notice](https://chesterbeatty.ie/about/who-we-are/), [Clonmacnoise notice](https://heritageireland.ie/places-to-visit/clonmacnoise-monastic-site/)

## Decisions and booking priorities

- Consider reserving **Kai for Saturday November 7**, one **Dublin dinner**, and **14 Henrietta Street or Kilmainham Gaol**.
- Decide whether the **Shed Distillery** deserves more of November 3, replacing some short stops or cycling.
- Decide whether to make room near Sligo for a **seaweed bath**, adjusting other sightseeing to preserve the Sligo arrival.
- Keep exposed coastal outings dependent on weather and daylight, and preserve the camper parking/overnight permissions still outstanding in the itinerary.
- Recheck venue hours, availability, menus and event arrangements before committing. None of these recommendations establishes motorhome access, clearance or overnight permission.
