Research reference for the November 2026 Ireland itinerary. Researched October 15, 2026.

## What we are looking for

Moyne Abbey near Killala is the benchmark: quiet, secluded, substantial ruins, beautiful surviving architecture, and a sense of discovery. Include derelict castles and ruins on private land as research leads, with access clearly distinguished from public visiting arrangements.

Route suggestions below are geographic planning judgments based on the saved Wanderlog itinerary, not verified driving directions. Camper access and parking remain unconfirmed unless specifically stated. Suggested stops are alternatives, not additions that all fit into the existing schedule. Access notices can change; check again before travelling.

## Highest-priority abbeys and monasteries

### 1. Ross Errilly Franciscan Friary — near Headford, County Galway

- **Why visit:** The strongest overall match for Moyne. A substantial complex with a bell tower, two cloister courtyards, domestic quarters, a bakehouse, and the kitchen's tank for keeping live fish. Enough survives to explore distinct spaces and understand the monastery as a place where people lived.
- **Setting:** Rural, with a narrow approach road. The OPW describes entry through a gate over a cattle grid.
- **Trip fit:** A deliberate diversion during the Galway stay or a changed approach to Galway. November 7 is tight before the market commitment; November 9 would require changing the direct route toward Roscommon.
- **Access:** OPW lists public access during daylight and a car park. Narrow-road access is explicitly noted; camper suitability is not confirmed.
- **Source:** [Heritage Ireland / OPW](https://heritageireland.ie/unguided-sites/ross-errilly-friary/)

### 2. Rosserk Franciscan Friary — Moy estuary, north of Ballina

- **Why visit:** The most natural companion to Moyne: substantial Gothic ruins beside the water, beautiful stonework, and a secluded setting. Look for the elaborate double piscina and the small round-tower carving on a pillar.
- **Trip fit:** **November 5**, especially if travelling from Downpatrick Head through Killala toward Ballina. A priority worth protecting time for.
- **Access:** OPW lists access during daylight. Camper parking remains unconfirmed.
- **Source:** [Heritage Ireland / OPW](https://heritageireland.ie/unguided-sites/rosserk-friary/)

### 3. Errew Abbey — Lough Conn peninsula, near Crossmolina

- **Why visit:** An abbey at the end of a lakeside peninsula, surviving ornamental stone windows, and a tiny separate “nuns' chapel” nearby. Particularly promising for seclusion and the feeling of discovering something tucked away.
- **Trip fit:** November 5 if choosing a route through Crossmolina. Requires a purposeful excursion along local roads rather than a quick roadside stop.
- **Access:** Present access arrangements, the approach across land, and suitable camper parking have **not been confirmed**. Treat as an access-check lead before committing to the diversion.
- **Source:** [Crossmolina Historical and Archaeological Society account, reproduced by Mayo Ireland](https://www.mayo-ireland.ie/en/towns-villages/crossmolina/history/errew-abbey.html)

### 4. Creevelea Franciscan Friary — Dromahair, County Leitrim

- **Why visit:** Substantial ruins beside the River Bonet, including a massive bell tower, cloister arcades, chapter house, and domestic buildings. Carved images of St Francis include him preaching to birds.
- **Trip fit:** A meaningful diversion east of Sligo. Fitting it into November 5–6 would require dropping another activity; it is not a minor stop on the direct coastal route.
- **Access:** OPW lists public daylight access and a car park. Camper suitability is unconfirmed.
- **Source:** [Heritage Ireland / OPW](https://heritageireland.ie/unguided-sites/creevelea-franciscan-friary/)

### 5. Burrishoole Abbey — near Newport, County Mayo

- **Why visit:** A ruined friary beside a tidal inlet, with a surrounding burial ground. An atmospheric waterside ruin that fits the existing journey particularly well.
- **Trip fit:** **November 4, between Westport and Nevin's**, just off the N59.
- **Access:** Listed as a visitor attraction by Discover Ireland. Confirm the immediate parking arrangement for the camper on arrival.
- **Source:** [Discover Ireland](https://www.discoverireland.ie/mayo/burrishoole-abbey)

### 6. Urlaur Abbey — near Kilkelly, County Mayo

- **Why visit:** A remote lakeside Dominican ruin with a surviving church and domestic remains. Local folklore tells of supernatural trouble and a piper summoned to help the friars.
- **Trip fit:** An option on the Boyle–Foxford leg if taking the more southerly approach. The confirmed **11:30 Foxford tour on November 4** limits time for a diversion and an unhurried visit.
- **Access:** Current entry arrangements and camper parking remain unconfirmed.
- **Sources:** [Abbey description](https://www.mayo-ireland.ie/en/towns-villages/kilkelly/history/urlaur-abbey.html), [The Friars of Urlur legend](https://www.mayo-ireland.ie/en/about-mayo/arts-culture/myths-legends/the-friars-of-urlur.html)

### 7. Abbeyknockmoy Cistercian Abbey — northeast of Galway

- **Why visit:** A substantial Cistercian ruin founded by the O'Conor king Cathal Crobhdearg, with a field-and-graveyard approach.
- **Trip fit:** **November 9**, close to the likely N63 route toward Roscommon. One of the strongest additions for the last camper day, subject to departure time and daylight.
- **Access:** OPW lists a car park near the graveyard, a short walk, and a stile over a fence using steps. Access is marked challenging. Camper parking dimensions are unconfirmed.
- **Source:** [Heritage Ireland / OPW](https://heritageireland.ie/unguided-sites/abbeyknockmoy-cisertican-abbey/)

### 8. Ballintubber Abbey — south of Castlebar, County Mayo

- **Why visit:** A medieval abbey with a restored, still-functioning church and an exceptional tradition of worship. It offers a different experience from the roofless ruins: a historic religious site still serving its congregation.
- **Continuous Mass tradition:** The abbey describes an **unbroken tradition of celebrating Mass since its foundation in 1216**, including **236 years when the congregation worshipped in the roofless building**. This should be described as continuous worship, rather than a verified Mass every single day or every Sunday for eight centuries. A claim that it is Ireland's longest such series has not been established by this research.
- **Trip fit:** A possible **November 4** stop on the Mayo drive. Located just off the N84 south of Castlebar, it requires a diversion from the direct onward route to Westport. Allow for the confirmed 11:30 Foxford tour and the planned arrival at Nevin's; choose among additional stops rather than assuming they all fit.
- **Current Mass times:** Sunday **11:00 a.m.**, Tuesday and Friday **7:30 p.m.**, as listed on the official site when checked October 15, 2026. Recheck the parish schedule before attending. November 4 is a Wednesday, so these regular times do not provide a Mass that day.
- **Access:** An active church welcoming parishioners and visitors, with guided tours also advertised. Confirm November visiting/tour arrangements and camper parking before making the diversion.
- **Sources:** [Abbey history and continuous-worship tradition](https://www.ballintubberabbey.ie/the-abbey/), [official Mass times](https://www.ballintubberabbey.ie/mass/), [location and visitor information](https://www.ballintubberabbey.ie/)

## Ruined castles and towers, including private-property leads

### Moygara Castle — near Lough Gara, southern County Sligo

- **Why visit:** A large fortified enclosure, or bawn, with corner residential towers and a gatehouse. A substantial rural Gaelic castle ruin.
- **Trip fit:** In the broader Boyle area, requiring a diversion. The November 3 camper handover and November 4 Foxford appointment constrain opportunities.
- **Access:** **Privately owned; arrange permission before entering.** The Heritage Council describes public access as a longer-term conservation aim, rather than an established visiting arrangement. The Moygara Castle Research and Conservation Group is a potential starting point for enquiries; nobody has been contacted.
- **Source:** [The Heritage Council](https://www.heritagecouncil.ie/moygara-castle-county-sligo)

### Deel Castle / Castle Gore — near Crossmolina, County Mayo

- **Why visit:** A ruined tower house with later additions beside the River Deel. A strong candidate for a secluded castle visit.
- **Trip fit:** Potential November 5 excursion if travelling through Crossmolina. Could compete with Errew Abbey for available time.
- **Access:** **Current entry arrangements unconfirmed.** Treat as a permission lead, not a confirmed public attraction. Nearby later estate-house remains should not be confused with the original castle.
- **Source:** [Crossmolina local history](https://www.crossmolina.ie/deel-castle/)

### Ardnaglass Castle — Ardabrone, near Skreen, County Sligo

- **Why visit:** Fragmentary but considerable tower-house masonry in an active field near Dunmoran Strand. A ruin that could easily be missed from the road.
- **Trip fit:** Particularly relevant to the west Sligo stay on November 5. Exact proximity depends on the onward route, which remains unresolved in the itinerary.
- **Access:** **Private farmland.** The Ó Dubhda Clan explicitly requests viewing from public roads unless the landowner grants permission. Livestock may be present; the ruin is not maintained as a public heritage site and is unstable in places.
- **Location:** Ardabrone townland, off the R297 between Dromard and Skreen. Do not confuse it with other Ardnaglass townlands.
- **Source:** [Ó Dubhda Clan: location, history, and visiting guidance](https://odubhda.org/ardnaglass-castle/)

### Roslee / O'Dowd's Castle — Easkey, County Sligo

- **Why visit:** A substantial ruined tower beside the sea and pier, with a strong coastal setting.
- **Trip fit:** November 5 if taking the coastal R297 route toward west Sligo.
- **Access:** An exterior viewing stop is the planning assumption. Current interior or upper-level access has not been established.
- **Sources:** [Easkey local visitor information](https://easkey.ie/attractions-activities-easkey/local-sights/), [Ó Dubhda Clan history](https://odubhda.org/rosslee-castle-easkey/)

### Garbally Castle — Skehana, County Galway

- **Why visit:** A dramatically broken tower house whose surviving walls expose its internal structure.
- **Trip fit:** A possible November 9 diversion south of the N63 corridor, potentially considered alongside Abbeyknockmoy. This is the Skehana ruin, not Garbally Court at Ballinasloe.
- **Access:** Entry arrangements and property boundaries need confirmation. Do not assume the interior is publicly accessible.
- **Source:** [Skehana and District Heritage](https://skehana.galwaycommunityheritage.org/content/places-2/heritage-experience/33-garbally-castle)

### Ballylahan Castle — near Straide, County Mayo

- **Why visit:** Lower, fragmentary castle remains rather than a towering shell, but an interesting discovery on a very relevant road.
- **Trip fit:** Convenient on the **Foxford–Castlebar N58 route on November 4**.
- **Access:** A brief exterior look is the planning assumption, only if there is a suitable stopping place. Site entry and camper parking remain unconfirmed.
- **Source:** [Straide Parish: places of interest](https://www.straideparish.com/places-of-interest/)

## Donegal cluster — near Dunkineely

These sites are shortly off the route toward Killybegs and Slieve League. Consider on **November 6 only if time remains** before the planned Ballyshannon arrival and evening concert.

### McSwyne's Castle

- Coastal castle remains overlooking Bruckless Bay.
- The local heritage group describes severe coastal erosion. Plan as a landscape-and-ruin viewing stop; this research does not establish safe interior access.
- **Source:** [Killaghtee Heritage Trail](https://www.killaghtee.ie/activities/thetrail/mcswyne)

### Old Killaghtee Church and Graveyard

- Ruined church, old burial ground, and the Killaghtee Cross.
- The local conservation group states that the council-owned site is open to all, reached along a short path through roadside wrought-iron gates. The ground is very uneven.
- **Source:** [Local conservation and heritage information](https://www.killaghtee.ie/activities/thetrail/old-killaghtee)

### Castlecarragh stone fort and nearby standing stones

- A nearby fort and standing-stone lead that complements the earlier folklore and ancient-sites research.
- **Private property:** the local heritage trail explicitly identifies both the fort and stones as being on private land. Permission is needed before entering; no owner has been contacted.
- **Source:** [Killaghtee Heritage Trail description](https://www.killaghtee.ie/activities/thetrail/mcswyne)

## Tempting sites currently on hold

### Rockfleet Castle — near Newport, County Mayo

A waterside tower associated with Grace O'Malley, in an excellent location for the November 4 route. However, the OPW currently states: **“Access is not permitted as conservation works are underway at Rockfleet Castle.”** Do not plan entry unless the official notice changes.

[Current OPW notice](https://heritageireland.ie/unguided-sites/rockfleet-castle/)

### Rindoon / Rinn Dúin — near Lecarrow, County Roscommon

An abandoned medieval town and castle on a Lough Ree peninsula, potentially a superb match for this hunt. However, Roscommon County Council currently states: **“There currently is no public access to Rindoon/Rinn Dúin.”** Older walking-route descriptions should not be treated as current permission.

[Council notice and history](https://www.roscommoncoco.ie/en/services/community/heritage/archaeology/rinn_duin-rindoon_deserted_medieval_town/)

### Menlo Castle — Galway

A picturesque riverside castle shell. Galway Tourism still carries a conservation closure notice dated January 2025. Reopening requires confirmation before planning an on-site visit. Its page also describes views from the opposite side of the river.

[Visitor notice](https://www.galwaytourism.ie/menlo-castle/)

## Recommended priorities for this itinerary

1. **November 4:** Burrishoole Abbey is the strongest natural fit. Ballintubber Abbey is a worthwhile deliberate diversion for its still-functioning medieval church and continuous-worship tradition. Turlough Round Tower and Ballylahan Castle are possible smaller stops depending on the chosen road and time after Foxford; choose among these options to preserve the Westport and Nevin's schedule.
2. **November 5:** Protect time for Rosserk. Errew and Deel are alternatives requiring access checks and a route choice through Crossmolina. The coastal Sligo route offers Roslee and the Ardnaglass permission lead. These cannot all be assumed to fit alongside Downpatrick Head and the Sligo arrival.
3. **November 6:** Keep the Dunkineely cluster as an optional stop; Slieve League and Ballyshannon timing take priority.
4. **Galway stay:** Ross Errilly is the top destination to make a special effort for. A visit requires allocating time away from the existing city activities or changing the arrival/departure route.
5. **November 9:** Abbeyknockmoy is the strongest fit toward Roscommon. Garbally is a possible additional diversion, subject to access and daylight.

**First private-property enquiries to consider:** Moygara Castle, Deel Castle, and Ardnaglass Castle. No permission requests or other external messages have been sent.
